## An important urban garden suburb

Tottenham appears in the Domesday Book (1086) under Lord Waltheof, son of Gospatric, Earl of Northumberland. Both names are commemorated in local streets: Waltheof Road, Waltheof Gardens, and Gospatrick Road all exist on the Tower Gardens Estate, with Lordship Rec's main gates directly opposite Waltheof Road.

During the 19th century's second half, Tottenham's population grew roughly tenfold. By 1900, it had become a village suburb connected to London by railway and tramline. The establishment of White Hart Lane Estate (now Tower Gardens Estate) and Tottenham's Urban District status in the 1920s consumed the last farmland. Today, Tottenham is part of the Borough of Haringey within Greater London.

### Development phases

Tower Gardens Estate was constructed in two main phases:

- **First phase (1899–1914)**: Initial development with 954 homes completed by 1914
- **Second phase (late 1930s)**: Northern streets including Gospatrick and Henningham Roads; De Quincey and Morteyne Roads built 1914–15; Topham Square developed in 1924 for families from poor Shoreditch housing

The estate holds historical significance as "one of the world's first garden suburbs," featuring low-rise, predominantly residential buildings with high architectural standards. Most was designated a Conservation Area in 1978.

### Architectural characteristics

South of Risley Avenue, W. E. Riley designed terraced houses in a rectangular grid with Arts and Crafts styling. North of Risley Avenue, G. Topham Forrest's post-1918 designs show Belgian influence, with lower density housing organised around Waltheof Gardens. The distinctive "butterfly" junction of Risley and Awlfield Avenues exemplifies Ebenezer Howard's Garden City principles from his 1898 work.

## History of social activities (1900–2004)

### 1900s

In 1903, the first house was built. The London County Council received land to create "quality housing and gardens" for working-class families from Tower Hamlets, despite local opposition. The estate became known as both Tower Gardens and White Hart Lane Estate.

### 1910s–1920s

By 1914, 954 homes were completed. Residents campaigned for a public hall, which the council refused to provide. The White Hart Lane Estate Welfare Association (WHLEWA), formed in 1919, organised whist drives, dances, sports clubs, annual sports days, and competitions. A monthly newsletter was distributed street-by-street through the 1950s.

### 1930s–1940s

The WHLEWA lobbied unsuccessfully for domestic electric lighting, baths, and hot water. By 1938, the Loan Club had 800 members and distributed £18,000 for Christmas. The 1936 public opening of Lordship Recreation Ground attracted 10,000 people. During WWII, a bomb struck The Roundway, killing at least one resident. The Waltheof Club became a community centre.

### 1950s–1970s

The WHLERA organised large sports days in 1952–53. The Morris House surgery opened in 1959 as an early purpose-built health clinic. In 1978, pre-1915 areas were designated a Conservation Area.

### 1980s–2000s

Right to Buy laws (1980) increased private ownership. A BBC programme titled "House and Home" aired in June 1985. The Tower Gardens Residents Network (TGRN), formed in 1998, campaigned for traffic calming and park improvements. By 2000, surveys identified traffic calming and youth facilities as top priorities. A £1 million Home Zone improvement programme ran 2001–2005. A residents association reformed in 2006.
